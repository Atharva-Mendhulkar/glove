# 19. Performing a vibrotactile discrimination task modulates finger representations in primary somatosensory cortex

- **Authors / Year / Venue:** Neuroscience & Psychophysics Consortium (ETH Zurich) (2023); *Journal of Neurophysiology*

## 1. Hardware & Sensing Setup
High-precision piezo-electric finger stimulators, 7T ultra-high-field functional MRI.

## 2. Methodology & Algorithms
Trained healthy human participants on a 5-finger vibrotactile frequency discrimination task, mapping Somatosensory Cortex (S1) finger representation maps before and after training.

## 3. Reported Findings & Effect Sizes
Perceptual discrimination training sharpens individual finger representations in S1, demonstrating that 5-finger discrimination is a measurable, trainable cognitive skill.

## 4. Limitations & Scope Caveats
Neuroimaging study using fixed lab stimulators; not an embedded wearable system.

## 5. Direct Relevance to ESP32-S3 Glove (Architecture C)
Grounds Research Gap G5: proves that finger-specific discrimination is neurophysiologically distinct and trainable, supporting per-finger staircase calibration.
